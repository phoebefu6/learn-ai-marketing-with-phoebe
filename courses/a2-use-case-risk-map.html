Part 1 · covers 2026 AI-marketing guides + CXL

Value vs risk: the scoring grid 7 min live

Every AI use-case gets two scores. Value is upside: time saved, output volume, quality lift, and reach. Risk is exposure: brand safety, disclosure and legal, and data sensitivity. Plot both on a 2x2 and the map tells you what to do first, what to govern hard, and what to refuse outright.

LiveWhy the risk axis is not optional3 min▶

Most teams score value instinctively and forget risk until something breaks. The risk axis is what separates a leader's map from a wish-list. It has three parts, and any one of them can move a high-value idea into "govern hard" or "refuse".

  • Brand safety. Could the output embarrass the brand, go off-voice, or make a claim you cannot stand behind? Customer-facing beats internal on this axis every time.
  • Disclosure and legal. Does it need an AI or endorsement disclosure (FTC/ASA)? Is it a comparative or performance claim? Fake reviews and fabricated stats are not high-risk - they are off the map, because they are illegal.
  • Data sensitivity. Does it touch customer PII, unreleased plans, or contracts? If yes, it only runs in approved tools with consented first-party data - rail two from a1.
Questions to ask your team "For each idea, what is the worst realistic headline if this went wrong - and would we survive it?" That single question surfaces the risk score faster than any rubric.
Self-studyScoring value and risk 1-52 min read▶

Keep it lightweight. Score each use-case 1-5 on value and 1-5 on risk, average the sub-factors, and plot it. Precision is not the point; a shared, comparable score across the team is.

Value (upside)Risk (exposure)
Time saved per weekBrand safety - could it embarrass us?
Output volume unlockedDisclosure / legal - claims, endorsements
Quality lift over todayData sensitivity - PII, unreleased plans
Reach or audiences servedReversibility - can we undo a mistake?

High value, low risk goes to the top-left and gets a green light. High value, high risk is a real opportunity you govern hard. Anything illegal - fabricated stats, fake reviews - never gets scored; it is refused.

Self-studyReading the pattern in the inventory2 min read▶
  • Internal and draft-stage work is low-risk. A blog draft you edit before publishing carries far less exposure than an autonomous decision that spends budget.

Part 3 · the sequencing rule

Sequencing: earn trust before you climb 5 min live

The order you adopt in matters more than the tools you pick. Start where value is high and risk is low and the work is internal or draft-stage. Build a brand-review habit while the stakes are low. Only then move to customer-facing work, and only after that to automated decisions. Each step earns the credibility the next one needs.

LiveWhy sequence, not sprint3 min▶

Teams that leap straight to the flashy use-case usually get burned and then over-correct into a ban. Sequencing avoids both failure modes. It builds a track record that makes the harder bets defensible - to your team, your legal partner, and your board.

  • Low-risk first builds the muscle. Drafting and repurposing teach the team to ground, edit, and verify - the same habit every later use-case needs.
  • The brand-review habit is the unlock. Once "AI drafts, a human owns the claim" is automatic, customer-facing work stops being scary.
  • Automation comes last, with guardrails. Automated decisions need budget caps, sign-off gates, and a kill switch. Earn them; do not adopt them to look modern.
Questions to ask your team "What would we need to see from our first bet - what result and what habit - before we would trust AI with something customer-facing?" Define the graduation criteria before you start.
Exercise

Score five bets and pick your first ◐ 15 min · bring to a3

Use the time-sinks you mapped in a1. No tools - just the grid and honest scores.

  • Pick five real candidate use-cases from your funnel. Include at least one drafting task and one that touches customer data or public claims.
  • Score each 1-5 on value (time saved, volume, quality, reach) and 1-5 on risk (brand safety, disclosure/legal, data sensitivity).
  • Plot them on the grid. Circle the one in the top-left - high value, low risk - as your candidate first bet.
  • For that first bet, write one line on how you would know it worked, and one line on the brand-review habit it should build.
  • Flag any idea that is illegal or off the map (fabricated stats, fake reviews) and strike it - do not score it.
Questions to ask your team "If we could only run one of these for the next quarter, which earns the most trust for the least exposure?" That framing usually names the first bet by itself.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What makes a strong first AI marketing bet?

Top-left of the grid: high value, low risk, internal or draft-stage. It delivers early wins and builds the brand-review habit every harder bet depends on.

2 · Why score the risk axis, not just value?

Value alone is a wish-list. Brand safety, disclosure/legal, and data sensitivity decide whether a use-case is a quick win, a governed big bet, or off the map (fabricated stats, fake reviews).

3 · What is the right sequencing for AI adoption in marketing?

Earn trust before you climb. Internal, draft-stage work builds the habit; customer-facing work comes next; automated decisions come last, with guardrails. Each step earns the credibility the next one needs.
