Part 1 · covers Harvard DCE + Wharton AI foundations

The state of play: past the hype and the fear 7 min live

Two stories have followed AI into every marketing team: the hype story ("it replaces the department") and the fear story ("it makes everything slop, or steals our jobs"). Both are wrong in the same way - they treat AI as a single magic button. The reality in 2026 is quieter and more useful: AI is a co-pilot across the whole funnel, strong in some stages, still supervised in all of them.

LiveThe co-pilot, not the button - AI across the whole funnel3 min▶

Think of AI as a very fast junior who never tires and never checks their facts. That description is unglamorous and exactly right. It changes how you lead: you are not buying a machine that does marketing, you are giving every marketer a co-pilot and then deciding where that co-pilot is trusted to steer.

  • It helps everywhere, replaces nowhere yet. Research, positioning, content, SEO and GEO, ads, email, social, analytics - AI adds leverage in each, and needs a human owner in each.
  • The strength varies by stage. It is strongest at drafting and ideation, useful for analysis and personalization, and still supervised for anything customer-facing or automated.
  • Agentic planning is arriving. Harvard DCE's 2026 material now teaches agentic campaign systems - AI that plans and sequences campaigns - as a governed future, not a turnkey product. We cover it properly in a6.
Questions to ask your team "Where in our funnel is AI already saving us hours - and where are we avoiding it out of fear rather than a real risk?" The gap between those two answers is your first opportunity.

Part 2 · covers Harvard DCE agentic framing

The three eras: assistive, augmented, agentic 6 min live

A simple ladder gives your whole team a shared vocabulary and stops the "are we behind?" anxiety. There are three eras, and the key insight is that capability and governance climb together - you do not earn the next rung by buying a tool, you earn it by proving you can control the last one.

LiveWhere most teams actually are - and how to climb3 min▶

Naming the era honestly is a leadership act. It replaces vague pressure ("we need more AI") with a clear question ("what would it take to earn the next rung safely?").

  • Era 1 - assistive: AI drafts, ideates, and rewrites; a human owns every output. This is where value starts and where slop risk is highest if you skip the review habit.
  • Era 2 - augmented: AI becomes a co-pilot for analysis and personalization - reading your data, tailoring messages to consented segments - still with a human in the loop.
  • Era 3 - agentic: AI plans and sequences campaigns with explicit guardrails, budgets, and sign-off gates. Emerging, not turnkey. Do not skip to it to look modern.
  • Most teams are in eras 1-2. That is not behind. Being deliberately in era 2 with strong governance beats a chaotic leap into era 3.
Questions to ask your team "Which era are we honestly in for each channel, and what one control would we need before moving up?" Make the answer specific per channel, not one label for the whole team.
Part 3 · the spine of the whole leader track

The leader's real job: decide and govern, not prompt 5 min live

Here is the reframe that makes this track worth your evening. Your job is not to become the best prompter in the room - your team will out-prompt you within a month. Your job is to decide where AI is worth the risk, protect what only you can protect, and build the conditions where AI creates leverage instead of liability.

LiveFive decisions only the leader can make3 min▶

Everything in the remaining five sessions maps to one of these five decisions. Prompting is a skill your team owns; these are yours.

  • Pick the bets (a2): score use-cases on value and risk, and choose the first one deliberately rather than chasing every demo.
  • Protect the brand (a3): own the voice, the claims, and disclosure - the things that cannot be delegated to a model.
  • Build the team (a4): roles, human-in-the-loop review, and the martech stack that supports it.
  • Measure honestly (a5): efficiency versus effectiveness, and resisting vanity metrics dressed up as ROI.
  • Sequence the roadmap (a6): the order you adopt in, so trust and governance grow ahead of autonomy.
Questions to ask your team "Of these five, which am I currently spending my time on - and which am I neglecting because I got pulled into doing the work myself?" Leaders drift into prompting; this list pulls you back.
LiveThe two rails you will govern to - stated plainly2 min▶

Two rails run through every later session. They are simple to say and they are where credibility lives or dies.

  • Rail 1 - own the brand and the claim. AI drafts; a human owns every word, image, and claim that ships. No fabricated stats, no fake reviews or testimonials, no unverifiable comparisons - and clear disclosure of AI-generated endorsements and material connections where the law requires it (FTC in the US, ASA in the UK). We go deep in a3.
  • Rail 2 - protect the data. Personalize only with consented first-party data, respect GDPR and PDPA, and never let customer PII or unreleased plans go into a consumer AI tool. Great targeting that breaks privacy law is not a win.
Real world

The shortcut that became a headline. Regulators have already fined brands for AI-generated and incentivized reviews presented as genuine, and for "AI-washing" their own capabilities. The pattern is always the same: fast, cheap, and exactly the shortcut AI makes tempting. The rails exist so the one thing AI cannot manufacture for you - trust - stays intact.

Questions to ask your team "If a customer or a regulator asked us to prove a claim in our next campaign, could we - today?" If the answer is a pause, rail one needs work before you scale AI content.
Exercise

Map your funnel's time-sinks ◐ 15 min · bring to a2

No code, no tools - just your own funnel and a clear head. This is the raw material you will score into a shortlist next session.

  • List your marketing funnel stages: research, positioning, content, SEO and GEO, ads, email, social, analytics. Use your real channel names.
  • Mark the top three time-sinks - the stages where your team spends hours on repetitive, drafting, or first-pass work.
  • For each, note in one line what an AI co-pilot could realistically help with, and one thing that would still need a human owner.
  • Flag which of the three touches customer data or public claims - those carry the two rails and will score higher on risk in a2.
  • Bring this one-page map to session a2, where you will score each candidate on value and risk and pick your first bet.
Questions to ask your team "If AI gave every one of us back three hours a week, which stage would we point it at first - and why that one?" Their answer often reveals the real bottleneck faster than any audit.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · A vendor pitches a "fully autonomous CMO" that runs your strategy unattended. What is the honest 2026 read?

In 2026 AI genuinely helps in every funnel stage, but the fully autonomous CMO is vaporware. Real deployments keep a human owner, especially anything customer-facing or automated.

2 · Which era are most marketing teams honestly in right now?

Most teams draft and ideate (era 1) and are moving into co-piloted analysis and personalization (era 2). That is not behind. Agentic (era 3) is emerging and governed, not the norm.

3 · What is the leader's real job in an AI-first marketing team?

Your team will out-prompt you fast. The leader decides where AI is worth the risk, owns the brand and claims, builds the conditions for safe use, and sequences the roadmap. That is the whole track.
