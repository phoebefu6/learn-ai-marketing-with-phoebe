Part 1 · covers Harvard DCE agentic-marketing material

The agentic-marketing future, realistically 6 min live

2026 curricula now add agentic campaign systems - AI that plans and assembles campaigns across steps, not just drafts one asset. It is real and worth preparing for. It is also over-sold. The leader's job is to hold both truths: prepare for governed campaign assembly, and refuse autonomous unsupervised spend. This area moves fast, so treat any specific claim as something to re-verify.

LiveWhat agents will and will not do in 24 months3 min▶

Draw the line clearly so your team is neither dismissive nor reckless. In a realistic two-year window, marketing agents mature as governed assistants that chain steps - not as autonomous marketers you hand a budget and walk away from.

  • Yes, plausibly: governed content and campaign assembly - an agent that drafts a multi-asset campaign from a brief, proposes a plan across channels, and prepares it for human review and approval.
  • Yes, with guardrails: assisting research, segmentation, and variant testing inside limits you set, always with a human sign-off before anything ships.
  • No, not responsibly: autonomous unsupervised spend - letting an agent move budget, publish, or make claims with no human in the loop. That is where brand, legal, and financial risk compound fastest.
Real world

The autopilot that overspent. Teams that wired AI directly to live ad budgets "to move faster" have watched it optimize toward the wrong signal and burn spend before anyone noticed. The lesson is not "agents are bad" - it is that autonomy without a gate is how a fast tool becomes an expensive mistake. Keep the human on the money.

Questions to ask your team

  • Where would we genuinely trust an agent to assemble, but not to ship?
  • What must always require a human sign-off, no matter how good the agent gets?
  • Which recent "agentic" claim did we take at face value that we should re-verify?
Self-studyHow to stay current without chasing every headline2 min read▶

The agentic space generates more hype than substance week to week. As a leader you do not need to track every release - you need a habit: re-verify any capability claim against a real trial before it enters a plan, and separate what a vendor demos from what your governance can actually allow. The roadmap direction is stable; the exact timing is not. Plan for the direction, verify the specifics.

Part 2 · the order that keeps you out of trouble

Sequencing the portfolio 6 min live

A roadmap is a sequence, not a wish list. Three phases, in order, each earning the next. The single most common failure is skipping the foundation to get to the exciting parts - and that is exactly how brands end up with content sludge, a disclosure incident, or a tool nobody governs.

LiveFoundation, then adoption, then transformation3 min▶
PhaseWhat you buildDone when
FoundationBrand-voice guide, tool tiers, team literacy, disclosure + privacy policyEveryone knows the rails and which tool is safe for which data
AdoptionCopilots per channel - content, SEO, ads, email, social - each with a baselineOutcomes (not volume) move on measured pilots that scaled
TransformationGoverned agentic workflows that assemble across channels with human sign-offAgents assist end to end within guardrails you trust
Do not skip the foundation The brand-voice guide, tool tiers, literacy, and disclosure policy are unglamorous and non-negotiable. Skip them and adoption produces sludge, transformation produces incidents, and the board loses trust in the whole program. Foundation is what makes the later phases safe.

Questions to ask your team

  • Which foundation pieces do we actually have written down, versus assume?
  • Are we tempted to jump to agentic before adoption is even working?
  • What is our honest current phase - and what does "done" look like for it?
Self-studyMapping your a5 business cases onto the phases2 min read▶

Each business case you draft (a5) belongs to a phase. Foundation cases justify unglamorous investment - a brand-voice guide, tool approvals, training. Adoption cases are your per-channel copilots with baselines. Transformation cases come last and only after adoption proves out. Sequencing the portfolio is simply ordering your one-pagers so each phase funds and de-risks the next, rather than betting everything on the far end.

Part 3 · the story that carries a board

The CMO narrative 4 min live

A roadmap does not fund itself - a narrative does. Boards back a clear story with honest numbers and named risks, not a feature list. The structure is short on purpose: where we are, the bet, the brand guardrails, the milestones, and the ask. It rests directly on the a5 business case.

LiveThe five beats and the board-slide outline3 min▶
  • Where we are. Honest current state - what AI is already doing for marketing and what it is not. No hype.
  • The bet. The sequenced 24-month direction in one line: foundation, then adoption, then governed transformation.
  • The brand guardrails. The a3 rails - own the claim, disclose, protect data - stated as strengths, not caveats. This is how you protect the brand while moving fast.
  • The milestones. The phase gates with the honest outcome metrics from a5, each with a decision point.
  • The ask. Budget, headcount, or a decision - specific, and tied to the first milestone.
Board-slide outline (one slide) Top: "Where we are" in a sentence. Middle: the three-phase strip with one outcome metric per phase. Left rail: the three brand guardrails. Bottom: the ask, with the date of the first decision gate. If it does not fit on one slide, the story is not sharp enough yet.

Questions to ask your team

  • Can we state our current position honestly, including what AI is not yet doing for us?
  • Are the guardrails framed as strengths a board can trust, or buried as caveats?
  • Is the ask tied to a specific milestone and date, or vague?
Self-studyTying the narrative back to the a5 business case2 min read▶

Your a5 one-pager is the first milestone made concrete: a real problem, a real cost, an honest expected value, and named risks. The CMO narrative zooms out from that single case to the two-year arc, but the credibility comes from the same discipline - honest ranges, named risks, and outcomes over output. A board that trusts one well-argued milestone will fund the sequence behind it. That is how the leader track pays off.

Exercise

Sketch your roadmap and CMO slide ◐ 30-45 min total

Turn everything from the leader track into one roadmap sketch and one board-slide outline. Keep it honest and sequenced - foundation first - and build it on the a5 business case you already drafted.

  • Draw the three-phase strip for your org: foundation → adoption → transformation, with a rough month range for each.
  • Under foundation, list what you actually need to write down: brand-voice guide, tool tiers, literacy plan, disclosure + privacy policy.
  • Place your a5 business case on the strip. Which phase is it, and what funds the next one?
  • Draft the CMO board-slide bullets: where we are · the bet · the brand guardrails · the milestones (with one honest metric each) · the ask.
  • Mark one claim about the agentic end of the roadmap that you would re-verify before promising it to a board.

Questions to ask your team

  • What is the one foundation gap that would most hold us back if we ignored it?
  • If the board funded only the first phase, would that still be a real win?
  • What would make us pause or stop the roadmap - and have we said it out loud?
