The AI-ready marketing team: roles, skills, and the change to lead

AI does not replace your marketing team - it reshapes what each person spends their day doing and rewards a different set of skills. This session is the leader's view of how the roles are shifting, the four skills worth building now, and the change moves that turn fear into curiosity without breaking your brand. No code. Part 0

Your team is the real AI strategy

It is tempting to think the AI question is "which tools do we buy?" The harder and more important question is "what do our people need to become good at, and how do we help them get there without fear or chaos?" The tools are commodities - your team's judgement, taste, and speed at using them is the moat. This session is about building that moat deliberately: naming the roles that are shifting, the skills worth investing in, and the change management that decides whether AI adoption feels like a threat or an upgrade.

Part 1 · covers CXL + HubSpot on AI roles

The roles are shifting 13 min live

Nobody's title has to change tomorrow, but the center of gravity of the work does. The grind of production moves to the machine; the value of the marketer moves up the stack toward strategy, judgement, and taste. Here is the shape of that shift.

LiveFour roles, one shift up the stack7 min▶

These are not four new hires - they are four kinds of work that need an owner, and often one person wears more than one hat.

  • The AI-literate marketer. Spends less time producing first drafts and more time on strategy, briefs, and judgement. The output per person goes up; the skill that matters shifts from typing to thinking and editing.
  • The brand guardian / editor. The person who owns voice, claims, and final quality. In an AI world this role gets more important, not less - someone has to be the taste and the truth check on volume.
  • Marketing ops + prompt libraries. The connective tissue: shared prompts, approved tools, and workflows so the whole team benefits from what works instead of each person reinventing it.
  • The creative / agency partner. Specialist craft and surge capacity. AI raises the floor on production, so partners shift toward the high-craft and high-scale work that still needs a human specialist - and toward being accountable for disclosure and rights.
Questions to ask your team Who owns each of these four today - and where is a role sitting in nobody's job description? Which of your best people are still stuck in production grind that AI could take off their plate?
Self-studyWhat does not change - and why that is the point2 min read▶

It is easy to over-rotate on what AI changes and miss what it makes more valuable.

  • Deciding what is worth making is still a human strategy call - AI cannot tell you what matters to your market.
  • Judging whether the work is good is taste, and taste is the scarce skill when everyone can produce infinite drafts.
  • Owning the claim and the brand stays with a person, always - the buck cannot stop with a model.
Questions to ask your team If AI took the production grind tomorrow, what would we want our best marketers spending that reclaimed time on - and are we set up to let them?

The skills to build 11 min live

Four skills carry most of the value. The good news for a leader: three of them are teachable to people you already have, and the most important one is a judgement skill your best marketers already have the raw material for.

LiveThe four skills, and how to build each6 min▶
SkillHow to build it
Editing + brand judgement (the #1 skill)Practice on real AI drafts - critique, cut, and rewrite to voice. This is coaching and reps, not a course; pair newer people with your best editor.
Prompt literacyShort internal workshops plus a shared prompt library. Learn by adapting prompts that already work for your brand, not from scratch.
Claim-verification disciplineA habit, not a class: every stat gets a source before it ships. Build it into the review checklist until it is automatic.
Data / analytics fluencyEnough to read results and question AI output honestly. Lightweight training plus making numbers part of every campaign retro.

Notice that editing and brand judgement sits at the top. When production is free, the marketer who can tell shippable from slop - and fix the gap fast - is worth more than one who can produce more of it.

Self-studyReskill vs hire - a leader's rule of thumb2 min read▶

Most of this is a reskilling problem, not a hiring one - which is cheaper, faster, and better for morale than assuming you need new people.

  • Reskill for prompt literacy, claim discipline, and editing judgement - your existing marketers already know your brand and market, which is the hard part.
  • Hire or partner for genuine capability gaps - deep analytics, specialist creative, or marketing-ops leadership you do not have at all.
  • Beware "hire an AI person" as a reflex. A single AI hire rarely fixes a team-wide skill gap; distributed literacy usually beats one specialist in a silo.
Questions to ask your team For each of the four skills, is this a reskill or a hire for us? Who on the team is closest to strong on the #1 skill - editing and brand judgement - and could coach the rest?
Part 3 · the leadership work only you can do

Change management + operating model 15 min live

Skills do not spread on their own. The difference between a team that adopts AI well and one that either fears it or misuses it is almost entirely change leadership - and then a clear decision about who owns what.

LiveFear to curiosity - the moves that work6 min▶

Fear comes from two places: "will this replace me?" and "what if I get it wrong?" Good change leadership answers both directly.

  • Name champions. Find the early adopters, give them air cover, and let peers learn from peers - it lands better than a top-down mandate.
  • Build safe sandboxes. A space to experiment with non-confidential data, where getting it wrong is learning, not a career risk. Fear of mistakes is what keeps people from trying.
  • Chase quick wins. One visible time-saving win beats a strategy deck. Let the team feel the leverage before you ask them to change how they work.
  • Make brand-review a badge of honor. Tie AI use to the quality bar from session a3, so "used AI well" means "passed brand review", not "cut a corner".
★ The reframe that unlocks a team AI is not here to replace your marketers - it is here to delete the grind so they can do the strategy, judgement, and taste work only they can do. Say that out loud, repeatedly, and back it up by protecting people who experiment in good faith.
LiveCoE vs embedded - who owns what5 min▶

There are two ways to organize AI capability, and the right answer is usually a blend that shifts over time.

  • Center of Excellence (CoE). A small central team owns tools, prompt libraries, training, and standards. Strong for consistency and governance early on; risks becoming a bottleneck if everything routes through it.
  • Embedded. AI capability lives inside each function - content, social, ads, email. Strong for speed and ownership; risks fragmentation and inconsistent brand safety without a shared spine.
  • The usual path. Start CoE to set standards and tools, then embed the capability into teams as literacy spreads - keeping a light central owner for tools, brand voice, and training.
What needs an ownerWho typically owns it
Approved tools + data safetyCentral (CoE) - one clear list, one standard
Brand-voice guide + prompt libraryCentral to define, embedded to use
Training + reskillingCentral to run, managers to reinforce
Claim verification + sign-offEmbedded in each function, per session a3
Questions to ask your team Are we CoE, embedded, or accidentally neither right now? Who owns tools, brand-voice, and training today - and if the answer is "nobody clearly", which of those do we assign first?
Exercise

Team skills-map + two change moves ◐ 30-40 min

Turn the session into a plan you can start this quarter. Map the skills honestly first, then pick just two moves - two done beats ten planned.

  • Build the skills-map. List your team down one side and the four skills across the top (editing + brand judgement, prompt literacy, claim-verification discipline, data fluency). Score each person green / amber / red. Patterns will jump out.
  • Mark reskill vs hire. For each red column, decide: can we coach our way there, or is this a genuine hire-or-partner gap?
  • Pick your #1 skill owner. Name the person strongest on editing and brand judgement, and give them a coaching role for the quarter.
  • Choose two change moves. From Part 3, pick exactly two to start this quarter - for example, a non-confidential sandbox plus a named set of champions. Write who owns each and what "done" looks like.
Questions to ask your team Where does AI feel like a threat versus a tool right now, and why? What is one grind task everyone would love AI to take off their plate this quarter? Who wants to be a champion - and what air cover do they need from me?

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Which individual skill matters most on an AI-ready marketing team?

When production is nearly free, taste is the scarce skill. The marketer who can judge whether AI output is good and correct it to brand voice is worth more than one who simply produces more of it.

2 · What is the strongest set of change moves to get a team adopting AI well?

Fear of replacement and fear of mistakes are what block adoption. Sandboxes make experimenting safe, quick wins prove the leverage, and champions let peers learn from peers - together they turn fear into curiosity.

3 · What is the core tradeoff between a Center of Excellence and an embedded model?

A CoE centralizes tools and standards for consistency but can become a chokepoint; embedding capability into each function is faster and more owned but can fragment brand safety. Most teams start CoE and embed as literacy spreads.
