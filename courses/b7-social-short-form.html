Part 0

Why social is a repurposing problem, not a writing problem

The slow way to do social is to sit down and invent a fresh post from nothing every single day. The fast way - and the better way - is to start from one substantial asset you already made, like the Cadence pillar blog from b3, and reshape it for each platform. AI is exceptional at that reshaping: same idea, different format, tone, and length for each home. Tonight you feel how one asset becomes a week, and you leave with a calendar you can rerun.

Part 1 · covers HubSpot Academy + content repurposing

One asset, many platforms 5 min live

The highest-ROI social move with AI is not writing more - it is repurposing. Take one pillar you already invested in, then spin it into posts fitted to each platform. A LinkedIn post is not an Instagram caption is not a tweet. Same core idea, five different shapes. You did the thinking once; now you get five outputs from it.

LiveRepurpose beats produce-from-scratch3 min▶

Producing an original post per platform per day is a treadmill you will fall off. Repurposing flips it: you invest once in a real asset, then let AI do the reshaping. The pillar carries the substance; each variant carries the format. That is why social gets cheap without getting shallow.

  • Start from substance. The Cadence pillar already has the argument, the proof, and the voice. Social posts inherit all of it instead of reinventing it.
  • Reshape, do not shrink. A good LinkedIn post is not the blog with words removed - it is the one idea from the blog, rebuilt for how people read on LinkedIn.
  • Volume from one source. One pillar can seed a week or more. You are multiplying an asset, not multiplying your workload.
★ Try it now (any chat AI) - one idea, three shapesHere is one idea: "most meeting notes are written for no one and read by no one". Rewrite it three ways: a LinkedIn post with a point of view, a punchy X post under 240 characters, and a warm Instagram caption. Do not reuse the same sentences across the three.
Real world

One webinar, a month of social. A small team ran one webinar, then repurposed the transcript into a pillar post, five LinkedIn posts, a dozen tweets, three carousels, and two short-video hooks. A month of social came from a single afternoon of real work. The reach was not from posting more often - it was from spending the effort once and reshaping it well.

Part 2 · covers HubSpot Academy social workflows

Platform-native, human-sounding 5 min live

Each platform has its own shape and its own norms, and the audience can feel a cross-post instantly. AI will happily produce content that is technically correct and totally soulless - engagement-bait openers, hashtag walls, "in today's fast-paced world". Your job is to keep it fitted and keep it human. Same core idea, platform-correct format, and a caption a person would actually write.

LiveWhat each platform actually rewards3 min▶

You do not need to be an expert on every platform to post well on it - you need a simple map of what each one rewards, and a rule to keep AI from flattening your voice.

PlatformWhat fits
LinkedInA clear point of view, a short story or lesson, plain text, one idea per post, a light question to close
X / TwitterA sharp hook, short lines, one thought per post or a tight thread; brevity and specificity win
InstagramVisual-first with a warm, human caption; a hook in the first line; a soft prompt to save or share
Short video (Reels / TikTok / Shorts)A hook in the first 3 seconds, one idea, spoken-word script, an on-screen text cue
The read-it-aloud test Before a caption ships, read it out loud. If you would never say it to a colleague - "let that sink in", "here's the tea", a wall of hashtags - cut it. A human voice is the one thing that survives the scroll.
Self-studyA repeatable de-slop pass for any caption2 min read▶
  • Kill the opener cliches. No "in today's world", no "let's dive in", no "game-changer". Open with the actual idea.
  • Trim hashtags. A few relevant tags beat fifteen. On LinkedIn, three is plenty; on X, often zero.
  • One idea per post. If it has two ideas, that is two posts. Focus is what makes short-form work.
  • Match the platform's length and rhythm. Do not paste a 300-word LinkedIn post into X. Reshape it.
  • Keep your voice. Feed the AI two real posts you like and say "match this voice". Generic is the default; your voice is the edit.
Part 3 · the habit that makes social sustainable

Cadence and consistency 4 min live

Social rewards showing up consistently far more than showing up perfectly. The trap is that daily inspiration does not scale - so you build a system instead. One source, many outputs, on a schedule you can actually keep. Consistency is a workflow, not a mood.

LiveOne brand voice across every platform2 min▶

The format changes per platform; the voice does not. Cadence should sound like Cadence whether it is a LinkedIn post, a tweet, or a Reel script - warm, clear, a little opinionated, never hype. That comes from the positioning and brand voice you locked in b2 and b3, carried into every repurpose prompt.

  • Anchor to the pillar's voice. The repurpose prompt should reference your brand voice rules, not invent a new tone per platform.
  • Vary format, not personality. A tweet can be punchier than a LinkedIn post and still sound like the same brand.
  • Consistency compounds. A recognisable voice posted regularly builds trust that a viral one-off never will.
LiveBatch, schedule, repeat2 min▶

The sustainable rhythm is batch-then-schedule: produce a week of posts in one sitting from one pillar, load them into a scheduler, and spend your daily energy on replies and comments instead of blank-page panic. AI makes the batching fast; the calendar makes it repeatable.

The one-source-many-outputs cadence Pick one pillar a week. Repurpose it into a week of posts in one session. Schedule them. Show up live only for the conversation. That loop is how one person keeps four platforms alive without burning out.
Demo 1 of 2

Turn the Cadence pillar into a week of posts ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Take one pillar and walk out with LinkedIn, X, Instagram, and a short-video hook - fitted to each platform, in Cadence's voice, none of it a copy-paste. This is the repurpose move in your own hands.

Open any chat AI. Paste your Cadence pillar from b3 (or a summary of it) plus one or two real posts whose voice you like, so the AI has both the substance and the tone to match.

Ask for the four variants in one go, each fitted to its platform: a LinkedIn post with a point of view, an X post or short thread, an Instagram caption, and a short-video hook script. Tell it not to reuse the same sentences across them.

Run the de-slop pass: cut engagement-bait openers, trim hashtags, keep one idea per post, and check each reads like a human wrote it. Read the captions aloud.

Verify any claim. Rail one still applies on social: if a post states a Cadence benefit or a number, it must be one you can back. If the AI reaches for a stat you did not supply, cut it.

Pick your favourite of the four and sharpen it by hand. That edited one is your anchor post for the week; the others support it.

Prompt A - the copy-paste trap (do not ship this)Here is my blog post. Turn it into a social post for LinkedIn, X, and Instagram. Add lots of relevant hashtags.
★ Prompt B - platform-native repurposeHere is a pillar blog about Cadence (an AI note-taker) and two example posts in my voice. [paste pillar] [paste 2 voice examples] Create four platform-native posts from ONE idea in this pillar. Fit each to its platform: 1) LinkedIn: a clear point of view, plain text, one idea, a light closing question. No hashtag wall. 2) X: a sharp hook then 2-4 short beats, or one post under 240 characters. 3) Instagram: a warm caption, hook in the first line, a soft prompt to save. 4) Short video: a hook for the first 3 seconds, then a 20-30 second spoken script + one on-screen text cue. Rules: match my voice from the examples, do NOT reuse sentences across the four, no engagement bait, no "in today's world" openers, and use only facts from the pillar.
Real world

The post that outperformed the ad. A founder repurposed one honest blog paragraph into a plain-text LinkedIn post - no hashtags, no bait, just the idea in their own voice. It reached more of the right people than the paid campaign running that week. Fitted and human beat polished and generic almost every time on social.

Demo 2 of 2

Build a one-week content calendar ★ 8 min · build your own

Four good posts are not a system until they are on a calendar. Here you turn this week's variants into a schedule, then capture the whole thing as a prompt you can rerun on any future pillar.

Give the AI your four posts plus your posting rhythm - which platforms, how many times a week, and any fixed slots (for Cadence, keep it realistic: a few posts across the week, not ten a day).

Ask for a one-week calendar table: day, platform, the post, and a one-line "why here" note. Ask it to space the platforms so you are not posting the same idea everywhere on the same day.

Add a repeatable-prompt request: "now give me a reusable prompt that takes any future pillar and produces this same week-of-posts-plus-calendar". Save that prompt - it is the real deliverable.

Sanity-check the calendar against your capacity. If you cannot keep it, cut it back. A schedule you actually hold beats an ambitious one you abandon in week two.

★ The calendar + reusable-prompt promptHere are four platform-native posts for Cadence and my posting rhythm: [paste the four posts] Rhythm: LinkedIn 2x/week, X 3x/week, Instagram 1x/week, one short video this week. 1) Build a one-week content calendar as a table: Day | Platform | Post | Why this slot. Space the platforms so the same idea does not land everywhere on the same day. 2) Then write me a REUSABLE prompt I can run on any future pillar to regenerate this whole flow - four platform-native posts in my voice + a one-week calendar - so next week takes minutes. Keep my de-slop and voice rules baked into it.
Why the reusable prompt matters The posts are this week's output; the prompt is the machine. Once you have a repeatable one-source-many-outputs prompt, every future pillar - a blog, a webinar, a customer story - becomes a week of social almost for free. That is the leverage of this session.
Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish both demos if you did not complete them live. The repurpose prompt is the one to get right - it is the engine you will reuse every week.
  • Take one real asset of your own (a blog, a talk, a customer story) and repurpose it into a week of platform-native posts using Prompt B.
  • Run every caption through the read-it-aloud test and the de-slop pass. Screenshot a "before" that sounds robotic next to your fixed "after".
  • Build your one-week calendar and, most importantly, save the reusable prompt so next week is a rerun.
  • Optional: pick one post to publish for real and watch which platform-native version earns the most genuine replies. Bring it to session b8.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What is the highest-ROI way to use AI for social?

The repurpose move - one source, many outputs - is what makes social cheap without making it shallow. You invest once in a real asset, then reshape it for each platform.

2 · You have a great LinkedIn post. What is the right way to put it on X and Instagram?

Platform-native beats copy-paste. Same idea, different shape: a sharp hook on X, a warm visual-first caption on Instagram. Identical text everywhere reads as a bot.

3 · How do you keep AI-drafted captions from sounding robotic?

Generic is the AI default; a human voice is the edit. Give it real examples of your tone, strip engagement bait and filler openers, and use the read-it-aloud test.
