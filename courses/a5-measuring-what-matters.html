Part 1 · covers the Wharton marketing-ROI lens

Efficiency vs effectiveness - do not count output 6 min live

AI collapses the cost of producing marketing content. That is efficiency, and it is real. But efficiency is an input, not a result. The failure mode is mistaking more output for better outcomes - and then reporting the output as if it were the win. Effectiveness is what actually moved: pipeline, conversions, retention, brand lift.

LiveThe content-sludge trap - and why volume can go negative3 min▶

When production is nearly free, the natural instinct is to make more. But "more" is not neutral. Mass mediocre AI content - what the industry now calls content sludge - dilutes your brand, trains your audience to skim past you, and increasingly gets discounted by search and answer engines that reward genuine expertise. The efficiency gain can quietly turn into an effectiveness loss.

  • Volume is a vanity metric. Pieces published, words generated, and impressions are inputs. They feel like progress and prove nothing about pipeline or brand.
  • Sludge has a cost. Generic AI content that ranks for nothing and converts no one still consumes review time, brand equity, and audience patience.
  • Fewer, better beats more, blander. The teams winning with AI use the efficiency to raise quality and free humans for strategy - not to flood the channel.
Real world

The blog that doubled output and halved traffic. A team used AI to quadruple its publishing pace, celebrated the volume, then watched organic traffic fall as thin posts buried the good ones and search stopped trusting the domain. The scoreboard said "winning". The business said otherwise. Output was the wrong number all along.

Questions to ask your team

  • What number are we actually celebrating this quarter - and is it an input or an outcome?
  • If we published half as much at twice the quality, what would we expect to happen?
  • Where might we already be adding sludge that dilutes the brand?

Part 2 · covers the industry pilot-to-scale playbooks

Pilot to scale, and buy vs build 6 min live

You do not need a transformation program to start. You need one measurable pilot: a single channel, a baseline, clear guardrails, and a date where you decide to scale, adjust, or stop. And before you buy anything, know which of three doors a tool comes through - because the real trade is cost against control and brand fit, not raw quality.

LiveThe pilot loop - baseline, guardrails, decision gate3 min▶

A good AI-marketing pilot is small enough to run in weeks and honest enough to trust. Pick one channel where you already have data, so you can compare like for like.

  • Baseline first. Write down the current outcome - conversion, cost per lead, retention - before you change anything. No baseline, no ROI story.
  • Guardrails from a3. Brand voice, disclosure, and data-privacy rails apply from day one, not after scale. A pilot that cuts those corners teaches the wrong habit.
  • One decision gate. Set a date and a threshold up front: "if conversion holds and cost drops X% with no brand or compliance incident, we scale". Decide in advance what "stop" looks like too.

Questions to ask your team

  • Which single channel has clean enough data to give us an honest baseline?
  • What is our stop condition - not just our scale condition?
  • Who owns the guardrails during the pilot?
LiveThree doors - buy, build, or blend3 min▶

Most tool decisions collapse into three options. None is "best" in the abstract - the answer depends on how much control and brand fit you need for that job, and how much cost and effort you can carry.

DoorWhat it isBest whenWatch-out
BuyMarketing-native tools (Jasper, Copy.ai, HubSpot AI, Canva)You want speed, brand kits, and templates without buildingData terms and lock-in - check what leaves your walls
BuildCustom or brand GPTs trained on your voice and assetsBrand fit and control matter more than time to launchUpkeep, governance, and someone must own it
BlendIn-suite copilots inside your CMS, ESP, ad, and CRM toolsYou want AI where the work already lives, low frictionGoverned by that vendor's terms - read them
The trade, in one line Buy trades control for speed. Build trades speed for control and brand fit. Blend trades independence for convenience. Pick per job, not once for the whole team.

Questions to ask your team

  • For this specific job, do we actually need brand-level control, or is speed enough?
  • If a tool shut down tomorrow, how exposed would we be?
  • Who owns and maintains anything we choose to build?
Self-studyWhy quality is rarely the deciding factor2 min read▶

Across the three doors, raw output quality is now broadly similar - most run on the same underlying models. What genuinely differs is data safety, brand consistency, workflow fit, and cost. Leaders who choose on a slick demo alone tend to relearn this the hard way. Choose on the trade that matters for the job, then let the pilot prove it.

Part 3 · the artifact you carry into a6

The business-case one-pager 4 min live

Budget follows a clear, honest argument - not a longer deck. A one-pager forces the discipline: one problem, one use case, a real cost, an honest expected value, the risks with their guardrails, and a specific ask. If you cannot fit it on a page, you do not yet understand it well enough to fund it.

LiveThe six blocks of a fundable one-pager3 min▶
  • 1 · Problem. The business pain in one sentence - not "we should use AI", but the outcome that is stuck.
  • 2 · Use case. The specific AI-marketing use case from your a2 map that addresses it.
  • 3 · Cost. Tools, time, and any build or upkeep - a real number, not "cheap".
  • 4 · Expected value. One honest outcome metric with a range, tied to the baseline. An effectiveness number, never output volume.
  • 5 · Risk + brand guardrails. The disclosure, brand, and data-privacy rails from a3, and what happens if they slip.
  • 6 · The ask. Exactly what you need - budget, headcount, or a decision - and by when.
Honesty is the credibility move A range ("we expect conversion up 5-12%, measured against Q2 baseline") beats a single confident number. Executives fund people who show their working and name their risks - not people who promise magic.

Questions to ask your team

  • Can we state the problem without saying the word "AI"?
  • Is our expected-value metric an outcome, or did an output sneak in?
  • What is the one risk that would sink this, and how do we guard it?
Self-studyHow this feeds the roadmap in a62 min read▶

One funded pilot is a data point. The roadmap session (a6) turns a portfolio of these one-pagers into a sequenced 24-month plan and a CMO narrative. The business case you draft tonight becomes the first concrete milestone in that story - which is why the expected-value metric has to be one you would be comfortable reporting against later.

Exercise

Draft a mini business case ◐ 30-45 min total

Pick one use case from your a2 use-case map and turn it into a one-page business case. Keep it honest - the point is an argument you would defend to a board, not the biggest number you can justify.

  • Choose one use case from a2. Write the problem as a stuck business outcome, in one sentence, without the word "AI".
  • Define its baseline: the current value of the outcome you will move (conversion, cost per lead, retention). If you do not have it, note where you would get it.
  • Set one success metric as an outcome, not an output - and give it a range, not a single hero number.
  • Name the cost (tools, time, any build), the risk plus brand guardrails from a3, and the exact ask.
  • Sanity-check: if a skeptical CFO read only this page, would they understand the bet and trust the numbers? Bring it to a6.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Your team's AI dashboard leads with "content published up 4x this quarter". As a leader, what is your reaction?

Volume is efficiency and a vanity metric. It proves activity, not results, and can even go negative through content sludge. Redirect the scoreboard to effectiveness: pipeline, conversions, retention, brand lift.

2 · What is the content-sludge trap?

Near-free production tempts teams to flood channels. Generic AI content buries the good work, trains audiences to skim, and loses search trust. Fewer, better beats more, blander.

3 · You are choosing between buying a marketing-native tool and building a custom brand GPT. What is the core trade-off?

Output quality is broadly similar across doors. The real trade is cost and control against brand fit and upkeep. Decide per job, and let a pilot with a baseline prove it.
